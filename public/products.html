Cislunar Orbits

Our tools provide accurate modeling of various CR3BP orbits of different classes, enabling mission planners to select appropriate satellite orbits for cislunar communication. Each of these periodic orbits (e.g., Halo Orbits, Lyapunov, Resonance, etc.) has specific characteristics and applications, often determined by their stability and mission objectives. These orbits are crucial for maintaining predictable lunar surface coverage in space mission design.

Trajectoris between Earth and Moon

We use invariant manifolds to design efficient trajectories between periodic orbits from Earth. In CR3BP, invariant manifolds play an important role in connecting different regions of space, providing pathways for low-energy transfers.

Communication Channels

We provide high-fidelity models for optical communication channels, allowing users to analyze the feasibility and performance of optical links between spacecraft and ground stations.

Multi-Relay Cislunar Communication

Users can simulate several types of multi-relay communication network architectures in cislunar space, each serving specific roles based on their unique characteristics: Orbital Relay Satellites provide communication between lunar assets and Earth; Lagrange Point Relays offer constant communication from stable positions; Lunar Surface Relays facilitate communication between assets on the lunar surface; High Elliptical Orbit (HEO) Relays maintain longer communication windows over key areas; Surface-to-Orbit-to-Earth (SOE) Relays ensure data reaches Earth reliably from the lunar surface.
